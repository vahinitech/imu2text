How it works

What the sensors measure

Two accelerometers, one at the front of the pen and one at the back, measure acceleration along three axes, including gravity: a pen at rest reads about 1 g. The gyroscope measures how fast the pen turns, the magnetometer the Earth's field as the pen's heading changes, and a force sensor how hard the tip presses. All 13 values arrive 100 times a second. None of them says where the tip is.

Seen and unseen writers

Unseen writers means no one in the test set wrote any of the training data. That is the test that matters for a pen meant to work for a new writer. Seen writers score higher, because the model has already learned their handwriting.

Why some letters are hard

Most mistakes are a letter read as its own other case: 43% of the errors of the 72.5% model. Scored without case, the same model reads 84.3%.

Many models, one answer

Five copies of the same network, trained from different random starts, vote by averaging their probabilities. When they disagree, the writer is unlike anyone in training; when they agree but split between two letters, the signal itself is ambiguous. The ensemble reads 74.46% against 72.18% for the average single model.
